\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{conclusion}


\section{Summary}

This thesis set out to build the SoH estimation foundation of a dual-model Digital Twin architecture for electric vehicle battery monitoring. The core problem was clear: accurate State-of-Health estimation on the vehicle's own microcontroller, under the tight memory and compute constraints of automotive hardware, with a model that remains accurate as the battery ages and operating conditions shift.

The solution developed here rests on three design choices. First, a multi-length window segmentation strategy trains a single model on data sliced at five different temporal resolutions, teaching it that the SoH label should be invariant to window length. Second, a compact ten-dimensional feature vector, computed from each window through physically motivated signal processing, decouples the model's input dimension from the raw sequence length. Third, LightGBM gradient-boosted trees, compiled to self-contained C code through the EDEN compiler and deployed on an SPC58EC80E5 automotive microcontroller, deliver inference through simple threshold comparisons and additions --- no matrix multiplications, no activation functions, no runtime dependencies.

The experimental evaluation on two public NASA battery datasets validated each of these choices. The LightGBM model matched or exceeded the accuracy of a BiLSTM baseline while running 11--30$\times$ faster on the same hardware. Its memory footprint --- under 3\% of the available Flash and 0.5\% of RAM --- leaves ample headroom for the BMS's other tasks. Most importantly, a single model, trained once on five window lengths, maintained stable accuracy on window lengths it was never trained on --- a property that BiLSTM architectures, with their fixed-length inputs, could not match.

The broader Digital Twin framework, in which a cloud-retrained SoC model is periodically refreshed over the air using the SoH estimate as a trigger, was designed at the architectural level. The SoH model itself is the foundation of this framework: without accurate, deployment-verified SoH tracking, the cascade architecture cannot function. This thesis delivered that foundation --- trained, compiled, deployed, and measured on real automotive silicon.

\section{Future Work}

Several directions naturally extend the work presented here.

\textbf{OTA implementation and end-to-end validation.} The OTA update mechanism was designed (Section~\ref{sec:ch3_ota}) but not implemented in hardware. A natural next step is to implement the full update cycle on the SPC58 platform: integrate the TCU communication stack, implement the A/B partition switching logic with signature verification, and test update reliability under realistic wireless channel conditions.

\textbf{SoC model integration.} Preliminary experiments revealed that SoH estimation errors propagate into SoC predictions when the two are cascaded. Closing this gap requires either improving SoH accuracy further (narrowing the error that propagates) or designing an SoC model that is explicitly robust to SoH input uncertainty --- for instance, by injecting synthetic SoH noise during SoC training. Either approach would bring the dual-model architecture closer to deployment readiness.

\textbf{Real-world data validation.} The NASA datasets used in this thesis were collected under controlled laboratory conditions. Evaluating the LightGBM model on data from fielded electric vehicles --- covering the full range of temperatures, driving patterns, and aging histories encountered in practice --- would provide a more definitive assessment of its robustness.

\textbf{Model quantization.} The deployed models use 32-bit floating-point arithmetic throughout. Exploring reduced-precision representations (16-bit or 8-bit integers) for the feature extraction pipeline and the tree parameters could further shrink memory footprint and inference latency. The SPC58's hardware FPU already provides efficient float32 execution; quantization would primarily benefit even more resource-constrained BMS platforms.

\textbf{Federated and privacy-preserving training.} The current design assumes centralized cloud retraining. A federated approach, in which model updates are computed locally on each vehicle and only aggregated parameters are shared with the cloud, would enhance data privacy, reduce communication overhead, and potentially capture a wider diversity of usage patterns across a fleet.

\textbf{Broader battery chemistries and pack-level estimation.} The datasets used here cover 18650 cells with LCO and unspecified Li-ion chemistries. Extending the approach to modern EV cell formats (prismatic, pouch) and chemistries (NMC, LFP), and to pack-level SoH estimation that accounts for cell-to-cell imbalance and thermal interactions, would increase its industrial relevance.
